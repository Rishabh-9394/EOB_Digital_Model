How the growth rule works

Every pair of cities starts connected by a faint tube of equal conductivity — a blank plasmodium with no preference. Each cycle, a batch of protoplasmic flows samples routes between cities, favouring tubes that are already thick and short.

Afterwards every tube evaporates a little, and the flows that completed short tours reinforce the tubes they used, in proportion to how short their tour was. Thick tubes get thicker, thin ones fade below the visibility threshold and disappear — mirroring how a real slime mould prunes redundant tubes down to an efficient network, as seen in Nakagaki's 2000 maze experiment and the Tero et al. Tokyo-rail study this algorithm is loosely modelled on.

Drag any city once a route exists — the tour re-lays itself over the new positions instantly; press "Grow network" again to re-optimise from scratch.
